Is it he or she?

Three years on, the four platforms no longer agree

Indonesian marks no gender on its third-person pronoun dia. Every English rendering therefore forces a choice the source never made. Each occupation was submitted as a matched pair — one clause praising competence, one describing carelessness — so the interesting cases are not only which gender a platform picks, but whether it picks the same one twice.

Share of the corpus receiving each assignment, 2023 against 2026. Parallel lines mean a platform stood still; crossing lines mean it changed its mind.

The split has a direction

When a platform gives the two clauses different genders, which clause gets which? Across every split in the corpus, the competent clause and the careless clause are not treated symmetrically.

Do the platforms track the study's own classification?

The 128 occupations were labelled masculine, feminine or neutral by the researchers. If a platform were merely mirroring occupational stereotypes, its feminine output would concentrate in the feminine-classified column.
